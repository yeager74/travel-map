2 · x100 / x1000 billed as REAL

Two different things scale, so two different rules:

WhatScales with R?Billing rule
Cylinders, magnetos, carburettor, alternator, vacuum, oil pump/lines, tyres, brakes, lamps, oil quantity (SPEC §2.2–2.3)yes, by RDivide the observed condition loss by R before pricing a service or repair — a part serviced after 6 minutes of sim time at x1000 is billed as the same 6 minutes' worth of REAL wear, not as 6 minutes × 1000.
Starter (SPEC §2.2: “the rate factor is capped at 100”)yes, capped at 100Divide by min(R, 100) instead of R — the wear model itself already treats x100 and x1000 identically for the starter, so the bill matches: the same starter cost at either setting.
Engine hours, tach, airframe hours, battery ageing/starts/hours/crank-seconds (SPEC §2.1, §2.5: “none” / “real time”)no, neverNo adjustment needed. The scheduled checks and the hourly reserves already price off real elapsed hours, so they are correct at any Wear rate setting without touching this rule.

3 · Reset to delivery, logged not billed

Detected the same way it's identified anywhere else on this card: NK_ST_MX_LOG advances together with the whole SPEC §2.2–2.4 seed pattern landing at once (ENG_HRS to exactly 300, parts to 85/60/70/80, oil to 7 qt, faults cleared, rates back to REAL). When that pattern is seen, the ledger writes one log entry — timestamp, the MX_LOG value, “Reset to delivery — test action, not billed” — into the tech log's closed-items history. It never becomes an invoice line, never touches the euro total or cost per hour, and (as settled in round 2) never touches the engine or propeller reserve funds either. The action is on the record; the money is untouched.

The key that makes any route safe — corrected in round 2, point 2. NK_ST_ENG_HRS is not safe alone: Overhaul sets it to 0 and Reset to delivery sets it to 300 (SPEC §2.1), so it can go backward on purpose. NK_ST_MX_LOG is the one variable that never does — it only increments, on every accepted page action, and Reset to delivery does not reset it (SPEC §2.4: “+1 (not reset)”). Pair it with NK_ST_TACH instead, whose own Reset column reads “not touched” (SPEC §2.1).

Store the last-seen (MX_LOG, TACH). Both higher than stored → an action happened, post it, advance the pair. Either number lower than stored — not only TACH with MX_LOG unchanged: a crash can reload both lower at once (say MX_LOG 12→11 and TACH down with it) — means the sim reloaded a save from before a crash. assumption (SPEC §1, and the spec's own hedge, not this card's): LocalVars save only on a normal exit, so a crash or killed process loses the changes since. On either number regressing: post nothing, and never reverse an invoice already posted. The book stops advancing until both numbers pass the last confirmed point again: no double billing, no silent refund.

For the kit stage, not the design: MX_LOG also counts rate changes and Reset, and a jump of more than 1 between two reads means more than one action happened at once. SPEC §4 already gives the way to tell them apart — a part at 100 is a service, oil at 8 qt is a top-up, ENG_HRS at 0 is an overhaul, a cleared FAULTS bit is a repair, a changed WEAR/FAILR setting is a rate change, and the full §2.2–2.4 seed set together is a Reset to delivery.
